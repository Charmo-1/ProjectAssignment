Introduction to Buhurt

Look at the origins, evolution, and rules of historical medieval battle. Understand how historical tournament traditions transformed into a new form of martial arts.

The Reality of Buhurt

Steel, Athletics, and Authenticity

Buhurt is not a theatrical renaissance fair performance. There are no scripts, pulled punches, and choreographed falls. It is a brutal, full contact sport where historical authenticity meets professional athletic training.

The name stems from Old French, translating roughly to a "tournament" Modern Buhurt brings back the exact structural chaos of 13th to 17th century tournament melees, organized inside a secure wooden fenced arena known as a lyst.

The Combat Breakdown

The 3 Point Ground Rule

The primary tournament logic is simple yet brutal: if you touch the floor with anything other than the soles of your feet, you are eliminated. A single knee, hand, or you being the only one of your team still standing ends the round with the opposing team as the victors.

Weapon & Armor Safety

All weapons are completely blunted, and edges must meet strict measurements and historical specifications that are usually tied to the 14th century. Armor must correspond structurally to historically verified armoring techniques from the 13th to the 17th centuries (depending on the time period specified), in museums and manuscripts worldwide.

What is Strictly Forbidden?

To keep athletes competitive safely, certain targets are heavily penalized. Direct stabbing with weapon tips, joint manipulation, strikes directly onto the spine, the groin, and strikes to the back of the knee carry the likelihood of immediate disqualification rules.

The Sport by the Numbers

Everything used in official leagues must be cross reference verified historical artifacts from either manuscripts with images or actual relics of the past. Fighters undergo intense training combining multiple elements of MMA. To begin, they are taught how to swing a sword, axe, mace, etc, alongside this they will use kickboxing, Judo, and any other style fit for armored fighting and grappling.

Armor Weight: Typically ranges between 60 to 80 lbs of tempered steel.

Weapon Edges: Must maintain a mandatory blunt radius edge of at least 10mm and a striking surface of 2mm thickness. They must be properly cared for and cleaned to prevent any rusting or cracking that might cause a fighter additional harm
